# CRTFE V0.3 P3 — Optimization and Scaling Campaign

**Revision:** P3 — 2026-08-25

## Mission

Determine and optimize the atmospheric plasma-flow operating region that can support a later low-slip traveling-field electromagnetic momentum-transfer test.

The program no longer treats V0.3 as a single scalar-conductivity gate. The test campaign maps how excitation method, geometry, flow speed, residence time and deposited energy control the spatial and temporal conductive state.

## Engineering question

What combination of plasma excitation, channel geometry, airflow and energy deposition produces the best repeatable combination of conductive volume, spatial uniformity, persistence and energy economy for subsequent electromagnetic coupling?

## Baseline retained

- 100 mm × 100 mm diagnostic duct as the initial instrumented article
- 10 / 20 / 30 m/s comparison points
- 20 / 50 / 80 mm multi-length impedance measurements
- 5×5 XY velocity traverse where instrumentation permits
- plasma-OFF baseline before energized operation
- synchronized electrical, thermal, optical and flow diagnostics
- ~60–150 S/m remains an engineering hypothesis/target region, not a measured result

## Campaign sequence

### P3-A — Instrument and flow baseline
Validate flow field, electrical noise floor, thermal baseline, pressure behavior and measurement repeatability.

### P3-B — Plasma-source screening
With the host laboratory and qualified operators, characterize available excitation families. Candidate scientific families include steady/pulsed DC, nanosecond-pulsed, inductive AC and capacitive AC. Methods are not assumed equivalent.

### P3-C — Operating-envelope sweep
Vary approved excitation settings, airflow, geometry and diagnostic position. Measure input energy, effective/spatial conductivity, temperature, optical state, flow disturbance and repeatability.

### P3-D — Persistence mapping
Measure downstream and temporal evolution. Replace reliance on one scalar conductivity with bounded σ(x,y,z,t) information to the extent supported by diagnostics.

### P3-E — Optimization
Rank configurations using a multi-objective scorecard:

1. conductive volume
2. spatial uniformity
3. persistence / decay time
4. repeatability
5. deposited energy per useful conductive volume
6. thermal penalty
7. filament/arc fraction
8. flow disturbance
9. compatibility with traveling-field coupling

### V0.4 — Electromagnetic coupling
Select the best characterized P3 operating point for the next-stage phase/direction-reversible electromagnetic force experiment. V0.4 requires plasma-only, field-only, hardware-only and combined-operation controls.

## Iteration rule

An underperforming configuration is diagnosed and used to choose the next controlled configuration. The program optimizes variables rather than treating one configuration as a verdict on the entire research direction.

Measurements remain falsifiable and are reported as measured. The optimization process does not redefine an underperforming measurement as success.

## Why this revision exists

External technical review identified that plasma energy deposition is strongly spatiotemporally dependent on excitation mechanism and that geometry and flow evolution are tightly coupled. A configuration-specific conductivity inference therefore cannot by itself establish general scaling behavior.

P3 directly addresses that criticism by making spatial/temporal characterization, excitation comparison, energy accounting and scaling the primary V0.3 outputs.

## Collaboration request

The ideal laboratory partner contributes:

- controlled airflow / duct access
- plasma-source expertise
- high-voltage/RF/pulsed-power qualified operators as applicable
- impedance/conductivity diagnostics
- optical and thermal diagnostics
- flow diagnostics
- experiment-design and uncertainty review
- safe test procedures and facility-specific operating limits

## Success path

P3-A → P3-B → P3-C → P3-D → P3-E → selected operating point → V0.4 reversible electromagnetic coupling → subscale propulsion validation → later vehicle-relevant scaling.

The target vehicle remains a long-range application and does not determine the experimental result.
