# CRTFE V0.3 Prototype Test Rig

> **Purpose:** Build a stationary moving-air diagnostic rig to determine whether atmospheric air can be made sufficiently conductive for the CRTFE traveling-field concept. This is **not an aircraft** and is not intended to lift a person.

## Goal

Measure, under moving-air conditions:

- `sigma_eff(t)` — bulk effective conductivity and decay
- ionization input power
- gas temperature / bulk Delta-T
- spatial uniformity versus filamentation
- conductivity lifetime / refresh rate
- downstream velocity or momentum coupling

The point of V0.3 is to replace the unvalidated conductivity assumption in V5 with measured data.

## Mechanical concept

```text
AIR IN
  |
  v
[ BLOWER ] -> [ FLOW STRAIGHTENER ] -> [ ENCLOSED PLASMA CASSETTE ] -> [ MEASUREMENT ZONE ] -> EXHAUST
                                                     |                         |
                                                     |                         +-- temperature
                                                     |                         +-- velocity
                                                     |                         +-- impedance / conductivity
                                                     +-- camera / optical view
```

Recommended first mechanical envelope:

- overall rig: about 1.2 m long x 0.25 m wide x 0.35 m high
- internal test duct: 100 mm x 100 mm
- flow-straightener section: about 150 mm
- plasma cassette: about 200 mm
- downstream measurement section: about 300–400 mm

## Simple build sequence

1. **Build the frame.** Assemble the aluminum extrusion into a long rectangular base and add rubber feet.
2. **Build the clear tunnel.** Attach polycarbonate panels around a 100 x 100 mm internal passage and seal ordinary air leaks.
3. **Install the blower.** Mount it at the inlet with a gasket and a finger guard.
4. **Install the straightener.** Put the honeycomb downstream of the blower with a screen on each side.
5. **Build a removable plasma cassette bay.** The plasma source should be a swappable enclosed module rather than a permanent part of the duct.
6. **Leave a long measurement section downstream.** Reserve about 300–400 mm for sensors.
7. **Install low-voltage sensors.** Place temperature probes before and after the plasma zone and an air-speed probe downstream.
8. **Install the conductivity-measurement interfaces.** Use a geometry compatible with the selected impedance instrument and plasma-diagnostic method.
9. **Install the safety guard.** The plasma-source compartment must prevent accidental access during operation.
10. **Install the control box.** Add the blower control, DAQ power, large E-stop, status lights, contactor, and enclosure interlock.
11. **Commission the rig with plasma OFF.** Verify blower operation, sensor readings, vibration, leaks, E-stop, and interlock behavior.
12. **Commission the enclosed plasma source only with a qualified operator and its manufacturer/laboratory procedure.**

## Safety boundary

The project does **not** include instructions for building a nanosecond/high-voltage pulser from scratch. Use a professionally enclosed source or institutional laboratory system.

Do not defeat interlocks. The plasma compartment must not be opened while energized. Keep HV power wiring physically separated from low-voltage sensor wiring and use an accessible disconnect/E-stop.

## Sensor locations

```text
BLOWER -> STRAIGHTENER -> TEMP-1 -> PLASMA CASSETTE -> TEMP-2 -> AIR SPEED -> IMPEDANCE ZONE -> EXHAUST
```

The camera should view the plasma zone through the clear wall. The visual record is diagnostic: a bright stringy discharge is a filamentary result and must be analyzed with V4 rather than averaged into an optimistic V5 bulk conductivity.

## Test sequence

### Test A — airflow only

Plasma OFF. Sweep blower speed. Record air speed, inlet/outlet temperature, and baseline instrument response.

### Test B — lowest stable plasma condition

Hold one airflow point. Qualified operator enables the enclosed plasma source at its lowest stable setting. Record input power, electrical diagnostics, temperatures, and video.

### Test C — conductivity decay

Measure conductivity/impedance versus time and downstream location. Extract `sigma_eff(t)` and decay lifetime.

### Test D — source-power sweep

Increase the source only within its rated operating procedure. Record conductivity, source power, Delta-T, and whether the discharge remains volumetric or becomes filamentary.

### Test E — airflow sweep

Repeat selected plasma conditions at several air speeds. Determine how conductivity and lifetime change with residence time and convection.

## Decision gates

| Measured result | Program action |
|---|---|
| Bulk sigma < 20 S/m | Stop / major architecture pivot for the current full-channel concept |
| 20–60 S/m | Borderline; determine the failure mechanism before redesign |
| >= 60 S/m | Re-run V5 using measured auxiliary power and thermal data |
| 80–150+ S/m | Preferred V5 region if real power/heating still close |
| Filamentary result | Use V4 failure model; do not use V5 bulk-conductivity assumption |
| Thermal/arc-only conductivity | Treat as a different hot-plasma architecture with the full thermal penalty |

## What happens after V0.3

Only if V0.3 demonstrates credible bulk conductivity at acceptable measured auxiliary power and temperature does the project move to the magnetic-thrust stage. That later experiment adds a controlled transverse/traveling magnetic field and a calibrated force measurement.

Do **not** buy aircraft-scale superconducting magnets before V0.3 answers the conductivity question.

## Current project interpretation

V4 remains the standing failure model for filamentary discharge. V5 remains the conditional traveling-field model for a genuinely distributed conductive volume. V0.3 decides which one applies to reality.
