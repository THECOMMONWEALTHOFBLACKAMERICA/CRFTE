# CRTFE V0.3 Prototype Test Rig — Revision 1.1

> **Purpose:** Build a stationary moving-air diagnostic rig to determine whether atmospheric air can be made sufficiently conductive for the CRTFE traveling-field concept. This is **not an aircraft** and is not intended to lift a person.

## Revision 1.1 changes

- Add a manual XY hot-wire traverse to measure `u(x,y,z)`, not a single velocity point.
- Make impedance measurement explicitly multi-length using at least three separations.
- Standardize first test flow to **10–30 m/s**. The vehicle reduced-order ideal induced velocity (~27.7 m/s) lies inside this envelope.
- Remove the unsupported `>30% efficiency` target. It is not a pass/fail gate.
- Add an explicit scale warning: the 100 x 100 mm duct is about 60x smaller in cross-sectional area than the 0.60 m² vehicle-module model.
- Reconcile the BOM to one canonical planning total.

## Goal

Measure, under moving-air conditions:

- `sigma_eff(t)` — bulk effective conductivity and decay
- ionization input power
- gas temperature / bulk Delta-T
- spatial uniformity versus filamentation
- conductivity lifetime / refresh rate
- **spatial velocity field `u(x,y,z)` and momentum footprint**

The point of V0.3 is to replace the unvalidated conductivity and momentum-coupling assumptions in V4/V5 with measured data.

## Mechanical concept

```text
AIR IN
  |
  v
[ BLOWER ] -> [ FLOW STRAIGHTENER ] -> T1 -> [ ENCLOSED PLASMA CASSETTE ] -> T2
                                                                        |
                                                                        v
                                              [ MULTI-LENGTH Z(f) SECTION ]
                                                                        |
                                                                        v
                                                [ XY HOT-WIRE TRAVERSE ] -> EXHAUST
```

Recommended first mechanical envelope:

- overall rig: about 1.2 m long x 0.25 m wide x 0.35 m high
- internal test duct: 100 mm x 100 mm
- first test-flow envelope: **10, 20 and 30 m/s nominal points**
- flow-straightener section: about 150 mm
- plasma cassette: about 200 mm
- downstream measurement section: about 300–400 mm

### Scale warning

The V0.3 duct area is `0.10 x 0.10 = 0.01 m²`. The vehicle reduced-order module uses `0.60 m²`, approximately **60 times larger**.

Therefore momentum spreading, turbulence structure, filament spacing, residence time, decay, and spatial uniformity measured in V0.3 are **screening data**. They must be re-validated at a larger cross-section before being treated as vehicle-scale V4/V5 inputs.

## Spatial velocity traverse

Do not use one fixed downstream anemometer reading as the momentum-coupling measurement.

Mount the hot-wire probe on a simple manual XY cross-slide/translation stage. Start with a 5 x 5 grid across the 100 x 100 mm plane:

```text
x = 10, 30, 50, 70, 90 mm
y = 10, 30, 50, 70, 90 mm
```

That produces **25 velocity measurements per axial plane**. If practical, repeat the grid at two or three downstream `z` positions.

For every plasma condition:

1. Measure the complete plasma-OFF baseline grid.
2. Repeat the identical coordinates with plasma ON.
3. Calculate `Delta-u(x,y,z) = u_ON - u_OFF`.
4. Preserve the full spatial field rather than only reporting a centerline number.

This is the V0.3 measurement needed to estimate the real momentum footprint and constrain the V4 `D_mom` / mixing assumption.

## Multi-length impedance fixture

The diagnostic section must contain at least **three sensing lengths**, rather than one two-terminal impedance pair.

Initial suggested axial separations:

```text
Pair A: 20 mm
Pair B: 50 mm
Pair C: 80 mm
```

Measure complex impedance `Z(f) = R(f) + jX(f)` for each geometry using calibrated instrumentation and fixture compensation.

Fit the real resistance contribution versus separation length. A true bulk-conduction contribution should produce an approximately length-dependent term. Fixed interface, fixture, and electrode effects primarily appear as non-length-scaling contributions/intercepts.

Do not label a raw two-terminal resistance as `sigma_eff` without this cross-check.

## Simple build sequence

1. **Build the frame.** Assemble the aluminum extrusion into a long rectangular base and add rubber feet.
2. **Build the clear tunnel.** Attach polycarbonate panels around a 100 x 100 mm internal passage and seal ordinary air leaks.
3. **Install the blower.** Mount it at the inlet with a gasket and finger guard.
4. **Install the straightener.** Put the honeycomb downstream of the blower with screens as required.
5. **Build a removable plasma cassette bay.** The plasma source remains a swappable enclosed module.
6. **Leave a long measurement section downstream.** Reserve about 300–400 mm.
7. **Install T1 and T2.** Put one temperature probe before and one after the plasma region.
8. **Install the three-length impedance fixture.** Provide 20, 50 and 80 mm sensing geometries compatible with the selected diagnostic instrument.
9. **Install the XY traverse.** Mount the hot-wire so its position can be repeated across the exit/measurement plane.
10. **Install the safety guard.** The plasma-source compartment must prevent accidental access during operation.
11. **Install the control box.** Add blower control, DAQ power, E-stop, status lights, contactor and enclosure interlock.
12. **Commission with plasma OFF.** Verify airflow, full velocity traverse, temperatures, impedance baselines, vibration, leaks, E-stop and interlock.
13. **Commission the enclosed plasma source only with a qualified operator and its manufacturer/laboratory procedure.**

## Safety boundary

The project does **not** include instructions for building a nanosecond/high-voltage pulser from scratch. Use a professionally enclosed source or institutional laboratory system.

Do not defeat interlocks. The plasma compartment must not be opened while energized. Keep HV power wiring physically separated from low-voltage sensor wiring and use an accessible disconnect/E-stop.

## Revised test sequence

### Test A — airflow baseline

Plasma OFF. Establish nominal 10, 20 and 30 m/s operating points. At each point, measure the full 5 x 5 XY velocity grid, T1/T2, and all impedance-fixture baselines.

### Test B — lowest approved plasma condition

Hold one airflow point. A qualified operator enables the enclosed plasma source at its lowest approved stable setting. Record source input power, electrical diagnostics, temperatures and video.

### Test C — momentum footprint

Repeat the identical XY grid with plasma ON. Calculate `Delta-u(x,y,z)` and map the spatial footprint. Repeat at additional downstream `z` positions if practical.

### Test D — multi-length conductivity

Measure `Z(f)` for all three sensing lengths. Use the length-dependent real-resistance contribution to estimate bulk response and reject fixed fixture/electrode artifacts.

### Test E — conductivity decay

Measure conductivity/impedance versus time and downstream position. Extract `sigma_eff(t)` and decay lifetime.

### Test F — controlled source/flow sweep

Repeat selected conditions only within the source's approved operating procedure. Record conductivity, source power, Delta-T, spatial velocity field, decay, and whether the discharge remains volumetric or becomes filamentary/arc-like.

## Decision gates

The previous unsourced `>30% efficiency` target is removed. The current gates are:

| Measured result | Program action |
|---|---|
| Bulk sigma < 20 S/m | Stop / major architecture pivot for the current full-channel concept |
| 20–60 S/m | Borderline; determine the limiting mechanism before redesign |
| >= 60 S/m | Re-run V5 using measured auxiliary power and thermal data |
| 80–150+ S/m | Preferred V5 research region only if real power/heating still close |
| Filamentary result | Use V4 failure model; do not use V5 bulk-conductivity assumption |
| Thermal/arc-only conductivity | Treat as a different hot-plasma architecture with the full thermal penalty |

## What happens after V0.3

Only if V0.3 demonstrates credible bulk conductivity at acceptable measured auxiliary power and temperature does the project move to the magnetic-thrust stage.

Before vehicle-scale conclusions, the spatial/momentum and conductivity behavior must also be checked at a larger duct cross-section because V0.3 is ~60x smaller in area than the modeled vehicle module.

Do **not** buy aircraft-scale superconducting magnets before V0.3 answers these questions.

## Current project interpretation

V4 remains the standing failure model for filamentary discharge. V5 remains the conditional traveling-field model for a genuinely distributed conductive volume. V0.3 Revision 1.1 now measures both the conductivity state and its spatial momentum footprint rather than assuming either one.
